Form For The Celebration Of The Lord's Supper (I)

Beloved in our Lord Jesus Christ,

Our Saviour invites us to His table today. He Himself instituted the Holy Supper, as the apostle Paul tells us:

"The Lord Jesus, on the night He was betrayed, took bread, and when He had given thanks, He broke it and said, 'This is My body, which is for you; do this in remembrance of Me.' In the same way, after supper He took the cup, saying, 'This cup is the new covenant in My blood; do this, whenever you drink it, in remembrance of Me.' For whenever you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until He comes."

Now if we are to celebrate this sacrament to the glory of God and the strengthening of our faith, we must first examine ourselves. This is what Scripture says:

"A man ought to examine himself before he eats of the bread and drinks of the cup. For anyone who eats and drinks without recognising the body of the Lord eats and drinks judgment on himself."

First of all, each one of us should ask himself if he is truly sorry for his sin. Both God's Word and this sacrament remind us that God is very angry at our rebellion and disobedience. Rather than leave it unpunished, He laid it on His beloved Son, Jesus Christ, in His bitter and shameful death on the cross.

Then also, let each one of us search his heart whether he believes the sure promise of God that, because of the bitter suffering and death of Christ, all his sins are now forgiven him. In faith he may appear before the holy face of God, clothed in the pure white garment of Jesus' perfect obedience, by grace freely given him as his own.

Finally, let each of us examine his conscience, to see if he is truly thankful to God for His marvellous gift of redemption.

Is there a true willingness to live before Him in love towards God and fellow man?

Is there an earnest desire to do all the good commandments of the Lord?

All those who are thus minded, God will certainly welcome as worthy partakers to this table of His Son. Those who do not have this testimony in their hearts, however, are warned in the name of the Lord. If they choose for themselves a lifestyle that excludes the Lord and do not want to repent, they should turn back to the Lord or else stay away from this table; otherwise they will eat and drink judgment to themselves and fall under heavier condemnation.

God does not take pleasure in the death of the sinner but rather that he turns to Him and lives. He therefore calls you and calls all men away from the ways of death [ as He describes them in His Word:

  • the worship of any creatures instead of Him, the only true God;
  • the trust in any powers instead of or alongside our heavenly Father;
  • disregard for God's holy name or God's holy day, His Word or sacraments;
  • disobedience to parents and other God-ordained authority;
  • hatred, jealousy and envy as well as drunkenness and selfish misuse of God's good gifts;
  • sexual immorality and the denial of love and faithfulness in marriage;
  • the love of money and of goods and possessions;
  • slanderous talk, untruthful words and deceitful actions. ]

Scripture says that all who walk in these ways with delight have no part in the kingdom of God.

When God tells these things to those who believe in Jesus Christ, it is not in order to make them think that only the perfect can come to the table of the Lord. It is sinners, not the righteous, who are called to repentance. When we are sorry before God because of our sins and shortcomings and seek in Jesus the life we know we do not have in ourselves, God welcomes us at the table of His covenant, where He says:

"I will be their God, and they will be My people."

And so this very sacrament is to strengthen us in our faith and in the resolve to do battle against the sin that still remains in us and around us. It calls us to rejoice in the wonderful grace whereby the Son of God

"...welcomes sinners and eats with them," calling and enabling them to "...go and sin no more."

Let us now consider the purpose for which the Lord has given us this Supper, namely that we should eat and drink it in remembrance of Him.

We remember that the Lord Jesus, true God from eternity, was sent by the Father into this world to become true man.

We remember that all the time He lived on earth He was burdened by our sin and God's judgment upon it.

We remember that in His agony in the garden He sweated drops of blood under the weight of our sins.

We remember that He was tied in bonds to set us free forever.

We remember that He suffered disgrace and ridicule to give us the dignity of being children of God for ever.

We remember that He, the innocent One, was condemned to death to have us, the guilty ones, acquitted at the judgment seat of God.

We remember Him who took upon Himself our curse that we might be filled with His blessing. He is the One who humbled Himself into the deepest desolation and agony of hell, when on the cross He cried: "My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?" This was done so that we might be welcomed by God and nevermore forsaken.

We remember how He confirmed with His death and the shedding of His blood the new and eternal testament, the covenant of grace and reconciliation, when He said: "It is finished!"

But we also remember that this Lord Jesus, after having suffered for us the agony of death, arose from the grave as the first-born of those who have fallen asleep, and ascended into heaven. We confess Him, ruling all things in glory and power, and as we sit around this table with the humble signs of bread broken and wine poured out, we do so in expectation of His coming again to restore all creation to praise and beauty.

At this table we have communion with Him, true God and true Man. We are members by grace of His Body, saved forever from sin and assured by the Holy Spirit of the victory that overcomes the world. That Spirit, who dwells in Christ as the Head of the Church and in us as members of the Body, gives us each day the life which we may live unto God forever.

And so we will not despair even when our faith is weak and our works fall so far short of God's standards, for we know that...

"...He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the Day of Christ Jesus."

At this table we also have communion with one another. The Holy Spirit who unites us to our Lord in glory also binds us together as believers, as the apostle says:

"Because there is one loaf, we, who are many, are one body, for we all partake of the one loaf."

We are one body, called to love one another from the heart, serve one another with our lives, and bear with one another in patience...

"...let us encourage one another — and all the more as you see the Day approaching."

Prayer

Merciful God and Father, whose grace abounds beyond all our sins, we pray that in this Supper in which we remember the bitter death of Your dear Son, You will so work in our hearts that we may yield ourselves more and more to Jesus Christ, our Saviour and our Lord.

Grant that our hearts, broken in true repentance, can rejoice in Your salvation, nourished and refreshed with Him, the true heavenly Bread, through the power of the Holy Spirit. Make us so one with Him that we no longer live in our sins but in Him, and He in us.

So confirm us in the covenant of Your grace that we may not doubt that You will for ever be our gracious Father, never again imputing our sins to us, and abundantly providing us, as Your dear children and heirs, with all things necessary for body and soul.

Grant us Your grace, then, that we may cheerfully take up our cross, deny ourselves, confess our Saviour, and in all temptations and trials expect our Lord Jesus from heaven, who at His coming will make our temporary, earthly bodies like His glorified body, and take us to Himself in eternity.

Through Him, our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom, with You and the Holy Spirit, belong all praise and adoration, now and forevermore. Amen.

The congregation is requested to arise. The minister indicates which of the two creeds to use, and says:

Let us now unite with the Church of all ages in the glad profession of the Faith once delivered to the saints, of which we speak with heart and mouth, saying:

EITHER

Apostles' Creed

I believe in God the Father Almighty,

Maker of heaven and earth.

And in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord.

Who was conceived by the Holy Spirit,

born of the virgin Mary;

suffered under Pontius Pilate,

was crucified, dead, and buried;

He descended into hell.

The third day He rose again from the dead;

He ascended into heaven,

and is seated at the right hand of God the Father Almighty;

from there He shall come to judge the living and the dead.

I believe in the Holy Spirit,

the holy catholic Church,

the communion of saints;

the forgiveness of sins,

the resurrection of the body,

and the life everlasting.

Amen.

OR

Nicene Creed

We believe in one God,

the Father almighty,

maker of heaven and earth,

of all things visible and invisible.

And in one Lord Jesus Christ,

the only Son of God,

begotten from the Father before all ages,

God from God,

Light from Light,

true God from true God,

begotten, not made;

of the same essence as the Father.

Through Him all things were made.

For us and for our salvation

He came down from heaven;

He became incarnate by the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary,

and was made human.

He was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate;

He suffered and was buried.

The third day He rose again, according to the Scriptures.

He ascended to heaven

and is seated at the right hand of the Father.

He will come again with glory

to judge the living and the dead.

His kingdom will never end.

And we believe in the Holy Spirit,

the Lord, the giver of life.

He proceeds from the Father and the Son,

and with the Father and the Son is worshipped and glorified.

He spoke through the prophets.

We believe in one holy catholic and apostolic church.

We affirm one baptism for the forgiveness of sins.

We look forward to the resurrection of the dead,

and to life in the world to come.

Amen.

The congregation is seated.

The minister then issues the invitation to communicant members and guests known to Session. While the table is prepared a psalm or hymn may be sung.

At the table, the minister shall say:

That we, then, may be nourished with Christ, the true Bread from heaven, let us lift our hearts to heaven, where He is, our Mediator, at the right hand of the Father. We must not doubt that we shall receive His body and blood as our food unto life eternal, through the gracious work of the Holy Spirit, as truly as we receive this bread and drink in remembrance of Him.

In breaking and distributing the bread, the minister shall say:

The bread which we break is the sharing of the body of Christ. Take it, eat, remember and believe that the body of our Lord Jesus Christ was given for a complete forgiveness of all our sins.

In serving the cup, the minister shall say:

The cup of thanksgiving for which we give thanks, is the sharing of the blood of Christ. Take it, drink from it, all of you, remember and believe that the precious blood of our Lord Jesus Christ was poured out for a complete forgiveness of all our sins.

While the elements are being distributed, or for a brief period following that, the minister or another member of the congregation may read fitting passages from Scripture, or a psalm or hymn may be sung.

Suggested readings for the thanksgiving at the conclusion of the day's celebration of the Lord's Supper:

Psalm 36:5-10.

Psalm 103:1-4; Psalm 103:8-13; Psalm 103:20-22.

Romans 5:8-10; Romans 8:31-39.

Revelation 4:11; Revelation 5:13.

At the conclusion of the celebration the Lord's Prayer may be said in unison or the following prayer may be used:

Almighty and gracious God and Father,

We thank You for the Son of Your love, who, as the Shepherd dying for His sheep, became to our hungry and thirsty lives the true Food and Drink to life everlasting.

We ask You to bind us to Him in ever-growing faithfulness and obedience, so that, strengthened by Your gifts of grace, we will be His witnesses at home, in the world, and to all creation.

Bind us then also to all members of Christ's Body, in this church and wherever we meet them, that in love and glad use of our gifts, we may work together for Your glory.

Keep us together as Your covenant people in the world, gladly bearing one another's burdens and advancing Your kingdom, until we sit with our exalted Lord at His wedding Supper, where our faith will be sight, our joy complete, and Your praise unbroken at last.

Through Him, our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom with You, Father, and the Holy Spirit, the one only God, be all honour and glory for ever. Amen.

The intercessions of the congregation for both local and universal needs may also be incorporated in this last prayer.

In smaller congregations, this prayer of praise and intercession around the table of the Lord could be led by more than one person at the direction of the Session.
