History of the CRCA

The Christian Reformed Churches of Australia (CRCA) includes over fifty congregations across Australia established since 1951 in just about every state and territory.

How the CRCA began

The CRCA was formed in 1951, after the post-war migration of Dutch people to Australia. From the start, the new denomination:

  1. Embraced and upheld Biblical and Reformed theology, as expressed in the ecumenical creeds and the Reformed confessions.
  2. Had a national profile that could enfold new settlers and others interested in every state and capital city.
  3. Was governed by a group of elders chosen periodically by the church and linked with representative state and national bodies.

The Reformed tradition

The Calvinist or Reformed stream of the Christian Church emphasises:

  • God’s honour, pre-eminence and grace in all things
  • Reconciliation with God through Christ Jesus alone
  • The work of the Holy Spirit essential for faith and growth
  • The Bible as the foundation of our faith’s substance
  • The church as a community in covenant with God

Since 1951

1951

The first congregations

The CRCA was formed as a result of the post-World War 2 migration of Dutch people to Australia. The first congregations were instituted in 1951: Penguin and Ulverstone in Tasmania on 13 October, and Sydney and Wollongong on 16 December.

1952

The first Synod

The first Synod met in June 1952 at East St Kilda, Melbourne, and adopted the name Reformed Churches of Australia.

1954

Reformed Theological College

Synod resolved to found a theological college to train ministers. Classes began in Geelong in April 1955.

1954–2010

Trowel and Sword

The first edition of Trowel and Sword was published by and for the Reformed Churches of Australia and New Zealand in October 1954, with the final printed edition distributed in December 2010.

Read about Trowel and Sword
1964

Cadets

The Cadet program for boys came to Australia, run by volunteers in local churches.

To the 1990s

Steady growth

Up through the 1990s the CRCA experienced steady growth and saw churches established throughout Australia.

2000

A new name

Synod 2000 changed the name of the denomination from the Reformed Churches of Australia to the Christian Reformed Churches of Australia.

Since 2000

Reforming to reach the lost for Christ

Since 2000 the CRCA has renewed its commitment to be a church that is reforming to reach the lost for Christ. It adopted a fourfold task (Pray, Train, Grow, Align) as the framework to expand the CRCA’s gospel impact throughout Australia. In every state there are renewed efforts to continue the pattern of the first CRC churches in Australia: to plant many new churches and also welcome migrants who are making their home in Australia.

2000s–2010s

Planting new churches

Established congregations planted daughter churches: Willetton in Perth planted Gateway, Grace and Connect; Kingston in Tasmania planted One Way, Bay, Summerleas and Redeemer; and Dandenong, One Hope and Casey together planted Pakenham Christian Community Church.
