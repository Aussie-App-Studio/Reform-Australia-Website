Form For The Solemnisation Of Marriage

Declaration of Purpose

People of God, dear friends.

We have come together in the presence of God to witness the marriage of Full Name and Full Name.

We want to honour the will of God for marriage, to seek His blessing on Full Name and Full Name, to surround them with our prayers and share in their joy.

Institution and Meaning of Marriage

Marriage was designed and instituted by God. In it a man and a woman covenant to live together in a life-long relationship of love, fidelity, companionship, and devotion to each other.

Right at the beginning of history, God declared, "It is not good for the man to be alone, I will make a helper suitable for him." "...A man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and they will become one flesh."

When man rebelled against God his Maker, one of the sad consequences was estrangement in the marriage relationship. But Christ Jesus came to reconcile man with God and in doing so also opens the door for people to find new life with one another.

The first sign Jesus did to show His redeeming power and glory was at the wedding of Cana in Galilee. Jesus endorsed the life-long nature of the marriage covenant when He declared that "what God has joined together, man is not to separate."

The apostle Paul sees the union of husband and wife as a symbol of the union between Christ and His church. In their sacrificial love, their faithful giving of themselves to one another, a husband and wife portray and imitate the love and faithfulness between Christ and His church.

Marriage is given so that husband and wife may enrich and encourage each other and find companionship in the wholeness of their life together in service to God. It is given so that they may love each other with delight and tenderness and through the joy of their physical union may deepen the union of their hearts and lives.

Marriage is also given to provide a secure environment into which children may be born and learn to know and serve the Lord. It is given so that home and family life might be strengthened, and that society might be enriched and stand on firm foundations.

When these purposes are faithfully pursued in union with Christ, His church is built, the kingdom of God is advanced and the well-being of husband and wife assured.

Marriage is nurtured in a relationship of mutual respect and love. As Paul says, "Submit to one another out of reverence for Christ."

In marriage husband and wife serve one another. A husband is to exercise responsible headship in love and a wife is to submit to her husband, both in a way which reflects the relationship of Christ to His church. Such leadership, given and received in love, acknowledges the gifts and responsibilities that both partners have. It also safeguards the freedom that husband and wife both possess before God.

Marriage is not to be entered into lightly or selfishly, but responsibly and in the deep awareness of the love and grace of God. Love, in Scripture, is more than a feeling: it is a commitment to one another in which we follow Him who loved us to the end.

It is in this Christ-like love that a husband and wife, following Christ together, will experience His daily grace. They will need that grace, just as they often have to forgive one another.

Our sinful and selfish tendency to break down what God has built threatens marriage with tensions and even broken bonds. However, God's grace in Christ will sustain them when trial, hardship and sorrow come to their home too, in our world still broken by sin. In such love, and by such grace, the life of two children of God in marriage — time and again — gives a true foretaste of the fellowship of everlasting life.

Invocation

O God, our Father, in Your great love for the world, You have given us the gift of marriage.

Bless Name and Name as they pledge their lives to each other. Unite them by Your Spirit so that their love may continue to grow and be a true reflection of the love of Christ for His church.

Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Declaration of Intent

The bridegroom and bride are asked to stand.

Full Name and Full Name, now that you have heard God's message concerning marriage, do you believe that it is the purpose of God for you to enter into this covenant and do you commit yourselves to fulfil your obligations to each other?

Each one answers:

I do.

PARENTS' SUPPORT (optional)

Both sets of parents are asked to stand.

EITHER:
OR:
Do you, Name of Groom's Parents, give your support and love to
Name and Name in their intended marriage and throughout their life together?

They answer: We do.

Do you, Name of Bride's Parents, give your support and love to
Name and Name in their intended marriage and throughout their life together?

They answer: We do.		parents could use the following or their own words in response and/or one of the parents could answer as representative for all:

Name and I, together with
Names sincerely and whole-heartedly endorse the joining of our children in marriage.

We promise them our love and support and pray that God will richly bless and use them in their life together and for the furthering of His kingdom.

CONGREGATIONAL SUPPORT (optional)

Will you who have come to celebrate this Christian wedding, uphold Name and Name their marriage, pray for them and support them as times and circumstances may require?

The Congregation answers:

We will.

Exchange of Vows

It is most meaningful for the bride and bridegroom to speak their own vows. Where this is inappropriate, they may be repeated after the minister. Their own formulation of vows may be used with caution — as long as their words then reflect the Biblical teaching of marriage.

GIVING OF THE RINGS (optional)

To pledge and symbolise your commitment to each other would you now give each other your rings.

Bride's ring is handed to the minister who gives it to the bridegroom. He places it on the fourth finger of the bride's left hand and says:

EITHER:
OR:
Full Name, with this ring I pledge my faithfulness and love.Full Name, I give you this ring as a pledge and symbol of the promises we have made today.

In either case the bride uses the same words when, in turn, she gives the ring.

Proclamation of Marriage

Before God and in the presence of us all, you, Full Name and Full Name, have made your solemn pledge to each other. Therefore according to the will of God, the laws of this land and the rites of the Christian Reformed Churches of Australia, I declare and pronounce that you are now husband and wife, in the name of God the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

Let no one separate those whom God has joined together.

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres."

Prayer and Blessing

The bridegroom and bride are asked to kneel and parents may be invited to stand around them in an attitude of prayer and blessing. Prayers are to be modified according to circumstances.

EITHER:
OR:
Father in heaven, give today Your blessing upon the marriage of Name and Name. We thank You for the vows they have spoken and the love in which they are now united.

Keep them faithful and strong in every trial. Sustain their joy and affection throughout a long life together.

We pray, Lord Jesus, that You will be acknowledged Head of their home and Master of their lives.

Equip them, Holy Spirit, with patient endurance, sacrificial service, unfailing courtesy, endless trust, and lasting love.

Grant that their home will often be a place of laughter; and in times of difficulty or trouble, a haven of healing and forgiveness.

May they and any children given them, constantly praise You, eternal Father, who with the Son and Spirit are God, blessed and exalted forever.

Amen.

Minister (and parents) extend hand(s) over the couple in blessing:

God the Father enrich you

God the Son make you holy

in His love

with His joy

The Lord bless you and keep you

and give you His peace.

All say:

Amen.		Lord of life, Creator and Redeemer,
We praise You for the gift of marriage, in which men and women may find fulfilment in each other.

We praise You for the Son of Your love, who, by His lonely and bitter sacrifice on the cross, restored to us the joy of the bridal song.

We now pray for Name and
Name as they have begun their life together. We thank You for the homes from which they have come, for the parents who have guided them so far. As they are now to establish their own home, we pray that Your grace may richly dwell there, together with the peace that passes all understanding.

Give them patience with each other and the strength to keep the promises they have just made. Make the relationship of Name and
Name with one another grow as their relationship grows with You, their God.

May their home be open to friend and stranger, and a true place of Christian nurture for any children You may give them.

Then, while bride and bridegroom remain kneeling, the minister shall extend his hands over them and bless them in the name of the Lord, saying:

"The LORD bless you and keep you;

The LORD make His face shine
upon you and be gracious unto you;

The LORD turn His face toward you

and give you peace."1

All say:

Amen.

At this time there is room for some celebratory symbolism beyond the traditional kiss. For instance:

A large candle could be lit from two small ones.

In the style of the Song of Songs, the couple could recite a verse to each other, whatever is culturally symbolic of union.

The parents and the couple could even symbolise leaving and cleaving.

A psalm or hymn is sung.

A Family Bible (or a devotion book) is presented on behalf of Session with a personal word by the presenting elder.

Signing of the marriage certificates is most meaningfully done in the presence of the whole congregation, after the benediction.
