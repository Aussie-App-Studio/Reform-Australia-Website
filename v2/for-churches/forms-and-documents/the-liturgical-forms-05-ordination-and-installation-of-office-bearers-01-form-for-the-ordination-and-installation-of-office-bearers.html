Form For The Ordination / Installation Of Office Bearers

General Introduction

Congregation of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Today we rejoice that God graciously maintains the special offices in His church. We thank God for those who have worked as office bearers amongst us and praise Him for providing others.

On two earlier occasions the Session announced that Names have been called to the office of minister/elder/deacon in this church. Seeing that no one has raised any lawful objections against their doctrine or life, we shall now, in the name of the Lord, proceed with their ordination/ installation.

Let us first listen to what the Word of God teaches regarding these offices.

Christ Himself, through His Word and Spirit, gathers, protects and preserves His church but in all this He uses the services of His people. The Lord is especially pleased to work through the members of His body, the church. All are called to be prophets, priests and kings with Him, but to some He has entrusted special tasks. The apostle Paul states that Christ appointed

"some to be apostles, some to be prophets, some to be evangelists, and some to be pastors and teachers, to prepare God's people for works of service, so that the body of Christ may be built up until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of the Son of God and become mature, attaining to the whole measure of the fullness of Christ."

In the office bearers and the work they are called to do, we see the love of Christ for His people. By His Spirit the Lord equips those who fill the offices for their tasks and through them enables the members of the church to grow in faith, develop mature Christian lives, serve others in selfless love, and share with all people the good news of salvation.

Christ taught the spirit of biblical leadership when He said:

"Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant, and whoever wants to be first must be your slave — just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life to be a ransom for many."

Office bearers, therefore, in all humility are to be servants of Christ and His people. Yet, they should do their work with the authority and dignity of Christ whom they represent. Jesus said,

"I tell you the truth, whoever accepts anyone I send accepts Me; and whoever accepts Me accepts the One who sent Me."

Particular Offices

  1. Ministers of the Word

Since the days of the apostles, God has called men to be ministers of the Word. This office is not higher or lower than that of elder or deacon, but each office has its own special tasks. Ministers are also elders of the church but their work is especially in preaching and teaching.

God commands them to "preach the Word, be prepared in season and out of season; correct, rebuke, encourage — with great patience and careful instruction ...keep your head in all situations, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, discharge all the duties of your ministry."

Such preaching has the two-fold object of calling upon sinners to be reconciled with God through Jesus Christ, and nurturing believers in the faith and life of the Kingdom of God. Whilst all believers are called upon to speak up for God, ministers of the Word especially are to be seen as Christ's ambassadors, as though God were making His appeal through them, "be reconciled with God."

Therefore the ministers of the Word are to see the faithful proclamation of the Word as their first responsibility. The execution of this task calls for a diligent and humble study of the Word of God. The ministry of the Word includes the preaching of the gospel to the congregation gathered for worship, the instruction in sound doctrine, especially of covenant youth and others who are yet to profess their faith in Jesus Christ. Ministers are also called to proclaim the gospel to those outside the Christian church in order that the elect of God may be brought to Christ. To this end ministers shall teach the members of the church to be faithful witnesses.

It also involves encouraging and admonishing, through the Word, the members of the church when they are visited in their homes. In all this the minister of the Word shall build up the church in the faith and knowledge of the Lord Jesus Christ. As a good shepherd watching over the sheep, he will challenge and resist the claims of the devil and refute false doctrine. He will call those who have sinned to repentance and faith.

To this end ministers shall also see to it that public prayer is said on behalf of all people and those who rule over them, so that "we may live peaceful and quiet lives in all godliness and holiness."

According to the command of Christ, ministers shall administer the sacraments, Baptism and the Lord's Supper, which have been instituted as signs and seals of God's covenant with His people.

Ministers shall be pastors to the Lord's people. With the Word and in prayer they shall comfort those who sorrow, rejoice with those who rejoice, strengthen the weak, visit the lonely, elderly and sick, and promote the biblical foundations of the Christian home and marriage.

Together with the elders, ministers shall exercise Christian oversight in the congregation and maintain good order and discipline. According to the command of Christ the keys of the kingdom are to be used faithfully so that "whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven."

From all this it is clear that the ministry of the Word is a most responsible task which none can do in their own strength. Therefore we look to our faithful Saviour and Lord Jesus Christ who is able to equip His servants for the work to which He has called them. Ministers themselves must also be devoted to prayer and the reading of the Scriptures for their own edification. They must be continually filled with the Holy Spirit, watch their life and doctrine closely, and fan into flame the gift of God which is in them.

So we rejoice that in His faithful love the Lord has provided us with a minister to serve as a pastor and teacher.

  1. Elders

The office of elder has been in existence since the days of the apostles. Elders were appointed in every church. This office is not higher or lower than that of ministers or deacons but each office has its own special tasks. The Word of God urges respect for the office of elder, and distinguishes between elders who labour particularly in preaching and teaching through the ministry of the Word, and elders who are charged with the supervision over the church. The elders of the church in Ephesus were called to keep watch over all the flock of which the Holy Spirit had made them overseers and shepherds of the church of God.

Elders are therefore to rule over the church in the name of Christ, the Head and King of the church. This implies that elders are responsible for the spiritual well-being of all members under their supervision. For this reason the elders shall faithfully visit the members of the church and discuss with them the privileges and responsibilities of serving Christ.

Elders shall have proper respect for the body of Christ and encourage the members to attend faithfully to the means of grace, the proclamation of the Word and participation in the sacraments. Elders are to admonish those who do not live in true fellowship with the Lord and, lovingly and patiently, call the stubborn and impenitent to repentance, using the means of Christian discipline where necessary.

Elders are called to provide for true preaching and teaching, carefully supervising the ministry of the Word and the sacraments, maintaining the public worship of the holy name of God, for the purpose of building up the church of God in the knowledge and love of Christ. They are to safeguard the church against false teachings. To do all this the elders shall study God's Word diligently and prayerfully so that they themselves, as good servants of Jesus Christ, grow in knowledge and experience of the truth of the faith and of good teaching.

Elders are also to extend the invitation of the gospel to the unsaved and through their own example as faithful witnesses urge the members of the church to be active in evangelism. To this end elders are to promote fellowship and hospitality among believers.

Furthermore, elders shall maintain good order and decorum in the church and all its assemblies. In all matters of doctrine and conduct they shall exercise supervision over one another, for God is not a God of disorder but of peace.

From all this it is clear that the office of elder is a most responsible task and no one can do it in his own strength. Therefore we look to our faithful Saviour and Lord Jesus Christ who is able to equip His servants for the work to which He has called them. Elders themselves must also be devoted to prayer and the reading of the Scriptures for their own edification. They must be continually filled with the Holy Spirit, watch their life and doctrine closely, and fan into flame the gift of God which is in them.

So we rejoice that in His faithful love the Lord has provided us with elders to watch over His people.

  1. Deacons

The office of deacon can be traced back to the time of the apostles when they appointed suitable members of the church to provide relief for the needy. Whilst they were not yet called deacons, it is apparent that in time the office of deacon became a permanent institution in the church of God. This office is not higher or lower than that of ministers or elders, but each office has its own special tasks. The Scriptures clearly indicate that the Lord has regard for those who cry out and for the afflicted who have no one to help them. He takes pity on the weak and needy. Christ Himself has compassion on the hungry and the thirsty, the stranger and the poor, the sick and the prisoner, and those who come to their aid are seen as doing this work for Christ.

Therefore deacons are to express Christ's love through the ministry of mercy. The work of deacons shall be governed by a spirit of compassion and kindness, love and cheerfulness. They shall stimulate relief in Christ's name for victims of poverty and distress, injustice and oppression.

To this end deacons shall encourage the congregation to take an active part in the ministry of mercy and support the diaconate with generous giving and prayer. Deacons are to promote faithful stewardship of the riches the Lord has given His people, urge them to practice hospitality, and to develop, where necessary, programs of assistance.

In the collection and disbursement of gifts required for their ministry, deacons shall act with integrity, discretion and wisdom, comforting those whom they help with the Word of God and prayer.

The task of deacons is to "do good to all people, especially to those who belong to the family of believers," always being ready to defend the rights of Him who is the Father to the fatherless and the Defender of widows.

Because life in God's creation is imperfect and bears the marks of the fall into sin, the reality of suffering is amongst us, but deacons may point all sufferers to Christ who knew what suffering was because He took up our infirmities and carried our sorrows. Deacons may comfort all believers who are afflicted by reminding them of the perfection of the kingdom of heaven in which, when it shall be fully established at Christ's coming,

"God will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the old order of things has passed away."

In their ministry of compassion, deacons shall rekindle in God's people the hope of things to come and remind them that:

"our present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that shall be revealed in us."

From all this it is clear that the office of deacon is a most responsible task and no one can do it in his own strength. Therefore we look to our faithful Saviour and Lord Jesus Christ who is able to equip His servants for the work to which He has called them. Deacons themselves must also be devoted to prayer and the reading of the Scriptures for their own edification. They must be continually filled with the Holy Spirit, watch their life and doctrine closely, and fan into flame the gift of God which is in them.

So we rejoice that in His faithful love the Lord has provided us with deacons to help us be a merciful and compassionate people.

Vows

Names, to express your acceptance of the office of Minister of the Word/Elder/Deacon, you are asked to stand, and here in the presence of God and His church to answer the following questions:

  1. Do you believe that, in the call of this congregation, you are being called by God Himself to serve in this holy office / these holy offices?
  1. Do you believe that the Scriptures of the Old and New Testament are the Word of God and that in them the way of salvation is taught completely, and do you accept the confessional standards of the Christian Reformed Churches of Australia as being in harmony with the Scriptures? Do you promise to defend this gospel and to reject all teachings that contradict it; to speak against all injustice and all untruth, and model for this congregation God's concern for compassion, justice and peace?
  1. Do you promise to carry out the work of your office(s) faithfully, to love the church and all its members, to live a life worthy of your calling and in submission to the government and discipline of the church?

Each office bearer answers individually:

By the grace of God, with all my heart, I do.

Laying on of hands or raising the right hand in the gesture of blessing:

May God, our heavenly Father, who has called you to your holy office, guide you by His Word, equip you with His Holy Spirit, and so bless your ministry that His church may increase, His kingdom be extended, and His name be praised. Amen.

Office bearers to remain standing, facing the congregation who also stand:

Congregation of our Lord Jesus Christ, to express your acceptance of these office bearers, you are asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Do you, before God and one another, receive this servant / these servants as your minister/elder(s)/deacon(s)?

Regarding ministers / elders:

  1. Do you promise to receive the Word of truth from them in love and obedience as they seek to give you leadership and guidance in the work of the Lord?

Regarding deacons

  1. Do you promise to enable the diaconate to function meaningfully in its ministry of compassion and mercy?
  1. Do you promise to pray for these office bearers, respect and encourage them in their work, and faithfully work together with them to the honour and glory of Christ, the upbuilding of His church and the extension of His kingdom?

Congregation, in response to these questions what is your answer?

All members of the local church answer:

By the grace of God, with all our heart, we do.

All are seated.

Charges

  1. To the minister:

I charge you, dear brother and fellow-servant in Christ: preach the Word; be prepared whether it is convenient for you or not; correct, rebuke and encourage — with great patience and careful instruction ...keep your head in all situations, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, discharge all duties of your ministry. Do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a workman who does not need to be ashamed and who correctly handles the Word of truth. Not lording it over those entrusted to you, but setting an example for the believers and all men in speech, in life, in love, in faith and in purity. Do not be ashamed to testify about our Lord, but take your share of suffering for the gospel, by the power of God who has saved us and called us with a holy calling. And when the Chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the crown of glory that will never fade away. Pray continually for the church.

To the congregation

And I charge you, people of God: welcome your minister in the Lord with great joy, and honour men like him. When you receive the Word of God through his preaching and teaching, then accept it not as the word of man but, as it actually is, the Word of God.

Thus the peace of God shall be upon you, and believing in Christ, you will be saved — you and your households. Pray for him, accept him, support and encourage him in all that he does as a servant of the Lord.

  1. To the elders:

I charge you, dear brothers and fellow-servants in Christ: keep watch over yourselves and all the flock of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, not because you must but because you are willing, as God wants you to be, eager to serve; not lording it over those entrusted to you but being examples to them. Be diligent in these matters, watch your life and doctrine closely. Persevere in them, because if you do, you will save both yourself and your hearers. Pray continually for the church.

To the congregation

And I charge you, people of God: respect those who work hard among you, who are over you in the Lord and admonish you. Hold them in the highest regard in love because of their work. Live in peace with each other. Obey your leaders and submit to their authority. They keep watch over you as men who must give account. Obey them so that their work will be a joy, not a burden, for that would be of no advantage to you. Sustain them in prayer, accept and encourage them as servants of the Lord.

  1. To the deacons:

I charge you, dear fellow-servants in Christ: Do your work with the same compassion as the Lord showed to those in need. When showing mercy, do it cheerfully. Help the needy and the afflicted, and have pity on the weak. Comfort them, both locally and elsewhere, with material support and the Word of God, with prayer and good advice. Encourage God's people to carry each other's burdens and in this way fulfil the law of Christ. Pray continually for the church.

To the congregation

And I charge you, people of God: acknowledge the deacons as ministers of God's mercy and provide the diaconate with generous contributions. Excel in this grace of giving, showing thereby the sincerity of your love. For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He became poor, so that you through His poverty might become rich. Sustain them in prayer, accept and encourage them as servants of the Lord.

It may be appropriate to have the newly ordained / installed office bearer(s) sign the in the service. This Form should then first be read out for the information of the congregation, and may be found on page 185.

Prayer

Our merciful Father in heaven, we thank You for providing faithful and gifted servants to minister to and build up the body of Christ. As these brothers assume the responsibilities of their office, we pray that You will fill them with the Holy Spirit.

We ask that You will equip them with wisdom and give them strength for their task. May they be faithful workers in Your vineyard. Under their guidance let Your church grow in every spiritual grace, in faith which is open and unashamed, and in the committed service that promotes Your reign in the world.

Deliver them from an attitude towards their office that is commonplace or self-assured. In their work grant them a humble dependence on Your grace, that they may come to You with all their needs.

Help us, Your people, to accept them gladly, encourage them always, and respect them for the sake of Your precious Son, our Lord and King, in whose name we pray.

Amen.
